Made for Effective range

Power plants

  • Springer / gas piston: one cock compresses a spring or gas ram. Self-contained but has a jumpy recoil that needs a soft hold.
  • CO2: cartridge-powered, mostly BB guns and pistols. Power drops in cold weather.
  • PCP (pre-charged pneumatic): filled from a scuba tank or pump to 3,000–4,500 psi. Most powerful, accurate and quiet; all big bores are PCP.

How MPBR is figured

A scope 1.5" over the bore, sight-in height given at 25 yards, sea-level air and a G1 model. Zones: 2" for pests and small game (head shots are smaller still), 4" for predators, 6" for deer-size big bores. Pellets slow down fast, so point-blank ranges are short.

Keep in mind

  • Big-bore airgun hunting for deer is legal in only some states; check before you hunt.
  • Projectile weights match real products checked in November 2026 (Daisy 5.1 gr BB; JSB Exact 8.44, 25.39, 44.75 and 81.02 gr; H&N Field Target Trophy 11.42 gr; Crosman Premier 14.3 gr). Big-bore speeds sit inside AirForce's published Texan range (.457 over 500 ft-lbs, .510 up to 700–800). Speeds are typical, not maximums, and vary a lot by gun and fill pressure. Pellet makers publish BCs on the GA drag model, which is not the same as G1, so the G1 BCs here are estimates.
  • Rule of thumb: about 6 ft-lbs at impact for squirrels and 12+ for rabbits and raccoons.
