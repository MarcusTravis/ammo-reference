Shotshell & Slug Chart

Common shotgun gauges and loads, plus slugs and pistol snake shot: what each is made for, payload and pellet count, speed, effective range and the choke to use. Slug point-blank figures assume iron or rifle sights about 0.6" over the bore, not a scope or red dot. Speed, energy and BC are typical figures for the listed load; real numbers vary by brand, lot and barrel length. BC only applies to slugs and buckshot. Tap a column header to sort.

Reading the load

Gauge is the number of lead balls of that bore size it takes to make a pound, so a smaller number means a bigger bore. The .410 is measured in inches instead. Payload is the shot weight in ounces; shot size gets smaller as the number goes up (#2 is bigger than #9). Shell length is the length after firing; the unfired shell is shorter because of the folded crimp.

Slug point-blank range

Only single-projectile slugs get an MPBR. It uses a 6" deer vital zone, open iron or rifle sights 0.6" over the bore, a G1 drag model and sea-level air, with sight-in height given at 50 yards. A scope or red dot sits higher and changes these numbers. Shot loads spread into a pattern, so their limit is pattern density, shown as effective range.

Keep in mind

  • Pattern your gun with your load and choke on paper before hunting; results vary a lot gun to gun.
  • Waterfowl require non-toxic shot (steel, bismuth or tungsten) by federal law. Never fire large steel shot through an old or unmarked full choke.
  • Check state rules for buckshot and slugs on deer, and for shotgun-only zones.
  • Velocities, pellet counts and slug BCs are typical figures from my own knowledge, not looked up. Check the box for your load.
