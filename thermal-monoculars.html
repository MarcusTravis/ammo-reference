Reading thermal monocular specs

  • Sensor resolution matters most: 256 is a close-range spotter, 384 covers most feeder and field hog hunting, 640 shows real detail past 200 yd, 1024 and 1280 are for open country.
  • NETD (in mK) is heat sensitivity. Lower is better and gives a cleaner picture in humid or foggy nights.
  • Lens size sets reach. Small 10–19 mm lenses give a wide view for scanning close; 35–50 mm lenses see farther but narrower.
  • Detection range is the maker's claim for spotting a man-size heat source, not for telling a hog from a calf. Plan on identifying game at a fraction of it.
  • A monocular is for scanning, not aiming. Most hog hunters scan with a monocular and shoot with a thermal scope.

How the ratings work

Popularity, image and durability stars are my own judgment from reviews (Outdoor Life's thermal monocular test, last updated 2025; American Rifleman; rifleconfigurator.com's 2026 coverage) and how common each one is with hog hunters. They are a starting point, not lab results.
