Made for

Iron sights only

Every point-blank number on this chart assumes standard open iron sights sitting about 0.6" above the bore, the typical height on a pistol or revolver. A red dot or scope sits much higher and changes the near zero, far zero and sight-in height, so these figures don't apply to optic-equipped guns.

How MPBR is figured

The bullet is zeroed so it peaks at the top of the target zone: 2" for .22 LR small game, 4" for .22 WMR, and 6" for everything else (a deer vital zone, or a 6" target for defense and practice rounds). Sight-in height is given at 25 yards, the usual handgun zeroing distance. G1 drag model, sea-level air, the listed load and barrel length.

Keep in mind

  • MPBR is how far the bullet stays in the zone, not how far you can hit with a handgun. Practical range reflects what a practiced shooter can do with iron sights.
  • Handgun velocity depends heavily on barrel length; a 2" snub can lose 100–200 fps or more.
  • Velocities and BCs are typical factory figures from my own knowledge, not looked up. Check the box for your load.
  • Check your state's rules for handgun hunting cartridges and minimum energy.
