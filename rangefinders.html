Reading rangefinder specs

  • Reflective range is the most a laser can read off a bright sign or rock. Deer range is what matters for hunting and is usually a third or less of the reflective number.
  • Angle compensation (HCD, ARC, angle-modified) gives the "shoot-for" distance on steep uphill or downhill shots. Every bowhunter should use it.
  • Ballistic solvers (Applied Ballistics, GeoBallistics, Hornady 4DOF) turn the range into a hold or dial number for your load. They only help if you enter your real velocity and BC and check them at the range.
  • Magnification: 5–6x is fine for bow and woods hunting; 7–8x makes it easier to hold steady on small targets far away.

How the ratings work

Popularity, ranging and display/glass stars are my own judgment from 2026 tests and guides (Outdoor Life's November 2026 rangefinder test, rifleconfigurator.com's 2026 guide, GunsAmerica, SIG and Bushnell specs). They are a starting point, not lab results.

Sources and limits

  • "Tested" figures are from Outdoor Life's 2026 test in Montana; others are the maker's claims, which assume ideal conditions.
  • Prices are US list or street prices seen in 2026 (maker stores, EuroOptic, Lancaster Archery, rifleconfigurator.com). Rangefinders go on sale often.
  • A dash means I don't have a confirmed number for that spec.
