8mm Mauser (8x57 IS) vs .30-06 Springfield

Typical factory loads side by side: speed, energy, BC, twist, range and what each one is built for.

The short answer

With typical loads, the .30-06 Springfield is about 300 ft/s faster at the muzzle (2,500 vs 2,800 ft/s).

The .30-06 Springfield carries about 6% more energy at the muzzle (2,872 vs 2,720 ft-lbs), which usually also means more recoil.

The 8mm Mauser (8x57 IS)'s typical bullet has the higher BC (0.56), so it holds speed better and drifts less in the wind at long range.

On the same size target, the .30-06 Springfield has the longer point-blank range (273 yd), so you can hold dead-on a bit farther without adjusting.

Which one fits you?

8mm Mauser (8x57 IS): German service round for both world wars.

.30-06 Springfield: U.S. Army service round turned the classic do-it-all hunting cartridge for any North American game.

For most hunters, the right pick is the one you can shoot accurately and comfortably, in a rifle that fits how you hunt. Good shot placement and a bullet built for your game matter more than small differences on paper.
