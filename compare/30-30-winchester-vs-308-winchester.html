Cartridge comparison

.30-30 Winchester vs .308 Winchester

Typical factory loads side by side: speed, energy, BC, twist, range and what each one is built for.

The short answer

With typical loads, the .308 Winchester is about 430 ft/s faster at the muzzle (2,390 vs 2,820 ft/s).

The .308 Winchester carries about 39% more energy at the muzzle (2,648 vs 1,902 ft-lbs), which usually also means more recoil.

The .308 Winchester's typical bullet has the higher BC (0.33), so it holds speed better and drifts less in the wind at long range.

On the same size target, the .308 Winchester has the longer point-blank range (266 yd), so you can hold dead-on a bit farther without adjusting.

Which one fits you?

.30-30 Winchester: Lever-action whitetail and black bear round for woods and brush shots.

.308 Winchester: Commercial version of the 7.62 NATO. All-around North American: deer, hogs, black bear, elk.

For most hunters, the right pick is the one you can shoot accurately and comfortably, in a rifle that fits how you hunt. Good shot placement and a bullet built for your game matter more than small differences on paper.
