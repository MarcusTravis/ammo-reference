7mm PRC vs 7mm Remington Mag

Typical factory loads side by side: speed, energy, BC, twist, range and what each one is built for.

The short answer

With typical loads, the 7mm Remington Mag is about 110 ft/s faster at the muzzle (3,000 vs 3,110 ft/s).

The 7mm PRC carries about 9% more energy at the muzzle (3,497 vs 3,221 ft-lbs), which usually also means more recoil.

The 7mm PRC's typical bullet has the higher BC (0.689), so it holds speed better and drifts less in the wind at long range.

On the same size target, the 7mm PRC has the longer point-blank range (306 yd), so you can hold dead-on a bit farther without adjusting.

Which one fits you?

7mm PRC: Hornady's modern long-range magnum built around heavy, high-BC 7mm bullets. Elk, moose, mule deer and sheep at long distance in a standard long action.

7mm Remington Mag: Long-range elk, mule deer and sheep with less recoil than a .300 magnum.

For most hunters, the right pick is the one you can shoot accurately and comfortably, in a rifle that fits how you hunt. Good shot placement and a bullet built for your game matter more than small differences on paper.
