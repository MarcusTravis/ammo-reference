.223 Remington vs .22-250 Remington

Typical factory loads side by side: speed, energy, BC, twist, range and what each one is built for.

The short answer

With typical loads, the .22-250 Remington is about 440 ft/s faster at the muzzle (3,240 vs 3,680 ft/s).

The .22-250 Remington carries about 29% more energy at the muzzle (1,654 vs 1,282 ft-lbs), which usually also means more recoil.

On the same size target, the .22-250 Remington has the longer point-blank range (284 yd), so you can hold dead-on a bit farther without adjusting.

Which one fits you?

.223 Remington: Civilian twin of the 5.56 NATO. Built for varmints and coyotes; deer only with 62–70 gr bonded bullets where legal.

.22-250 Remington: Purpose-built high-speed varmint and predator round: prairie dogs, coyotes, foxes.

For most hunters, the right pick is the one you can shoot accurately and comfortably, in a rifle that fits how you hunt. Good shot placement and a bullet built for your game matter more than small differences on paper.
