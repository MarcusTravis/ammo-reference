Cartridge comparison

.38 Special vs .357 Magnum

Typical factory loads side by side: speed, energy, BC, twist, range and what each one is built for.

The short answer

With typical loads, the .357 Magnum is about 480 ft/s faster at the muzzle (755 vs 1,235 ft/s).

The .357 Magnum carries about 167% more energy at the muzzle (535 vs 200 ft-lbs), which usually also means more recoil.

On the same size target, the .357 Magnum has the longer point-blank range (115 yd), so you can hold dead-on a bit farther without adjusting.

Which one fits you?

.38 Special: Classic revolver round for police, self-defense and target shooting. Mild recoil; also fires in any .357 Magnum.

.357 Magnum: Lengthened .38 Special at much higher pressure. Self-defense, deer and hogs at short range from a 6" revolver.

For most hunters, the right pick is the one you can shoot accurately and comfortably, in a rifle that fits how you hunt. Good shot placement and a bullet built for your game matter more than small differences on paper.
