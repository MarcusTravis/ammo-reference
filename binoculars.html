Reading binocular specs

  • 8x42 vs 10x42: 8x is steadier with a wider view for woods and brush; 10x shows more detail across fields and canyons. 12x and up usually need a tripod or a stabilized bino.
  • Exit pupil (objective ÷ power) is how much light reaches your eye. 4–5 mm or more helps at dawn and dusk.
  • Field of view is how wide a slice you see at 1,000 yd. Wider makes it easier to find and follow game.
  • Weight adds up on a long hunt. Most 10x42s weigh 21–30 oz; a chest harness makes any of them easier to carry.

How the ratings work

Popularity, glass and build stars are my own judgment from 2026 tests (Outdoor Life's hunting binocular test, Field & Stream) and how common each model is with hunters. They are a starting point, not lab results.

Sources and limits

  • Field of view, close focus and weight were checked model by model against each maker's spec page in November 2026 (Bushnell's own page lists 8x42 numbers for the R5 10x42, so that row uses retailer spec sheets). A dash means no confirmed number.
  • Prices are US list prices from maker stores in November 2026, with a street low where retailers sell for less. Vortex binos are often well below list on sale.
  • "Warranty not confirmed" means the source didn't state it; check the maker before buying.
